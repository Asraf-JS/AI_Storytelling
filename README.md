## The dataset

All of it is synthetic. There is **no real data** in this file, so it is
completely safe to paste into Copilot or Claude.

Four business domains, eleven data sheets:

| Domain | Sheets | What is in them |
|---|---|---|
| **Clinical** | `Study_Master`, `Site_Portfolio`, `Site_Query_Log`, `Site_Payments` | 40 studies, 530 sites, queries and payments |
| **Finance** | `P2P`, `O2C`, `GA` | Vendor invoices, receivables, journal entries |
| **HR** | `HR` | Headcount, tenure, attrition, engagement |
| **Compliance** | `Compliance` | Audit findings, severity, ageing |
| **Lab 4 only** | `P2P_Raw`, `HR_Raw` | Deliberately messy versions. You will clean one of these |

**Open the `Data Dictionary` tab first.** It defines every column and lists decision
questions for each domain. If you are ever unsure what to investigate, take a question
from there rather than inventing one.

You will pick one domain as your path and stay in it for most of the programme. Choose
the one closest to your own work.

## The one thing to know in advance

Everything in this programme sits on one idea called **BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front**.
Four layers, always in this order:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Headline | What decision does this data support? One sentence, with a number in it |
| **2** | Context | Why should this audience care? |
| **3** | Evidence | What do the numbers show? Three to five data points |
| **4** | Action | Who does what, by when? |

You write it in reverse — look at the data, work out what it means, decide what to do,
then write the headline last. But the reader sees the headline first.

That is the whole framework. Everything else is practice.

## Yes, you will present

On day 2 everyone delivers a two-minute decision brief to the group. Standing, with
one chart.

Before that happens you rehearse it twice, in Lab 7 and again in Lab 10. Nobody
presents cold, and it is consistently the part people say afterwards was most useful.

## When you get stuck

Two things fix most problems:

1. **Your prompt is probably too short.** Name the sheet, list the columns, say what
   you want, and say what shape you want the answer back in.
2. **On `P2P_Raw` and `HR_Raw` the real headers are on row 4, not row 1.** Nearly every
   formula error on those sheets traces back to this.

Beyond that, the lab manual has a troubleshooting table for each lab, and the prompt
library has one covering AI answers that come back wrong.

And put your hand up. Being stuck is not a problem. Staying stuck quietly is.

## After the programme

Three things worth keeping:

- **The prompt library.** Adapt the column names to your own data and it works
  immediately at your desk.
- **The blank BLUF template** at the back of the workbook. Use it on your next real
  report.
- **Your thirty-day commitment.** BLUF stops feeling deliberate after about a month of
  use. Apply it once this week, ask a colleague whether it is clear enough to act on,
  and go from there.
